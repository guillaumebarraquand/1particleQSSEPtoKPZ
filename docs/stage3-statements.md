## How to read the statements

The conventions of Stage 2 (`docs/stage2-statements.md`) apply: arbitrary filtered probability space without usual conditions, Brownian family $B$, wave function $\psi$ on $\mathbb Z$ (`IsWaveFunction ⊤`), $P(t,x)=\lvert\psi_t(x)\rvert^2$, the filtration $\mathcal F^n_t=\mathcal F_{nt/2}$; constants "depending only on" some parameters are chosen before the probability space; every series, integral and law that appears is asserted to be genuine, so that no statement can hold because of a convention of Lean (a divergent series has sum $0$ in Lean, the image of a measure under a map that is not almost everywhere measurable is an arbitrary Dirac mass instead of the law, and so on).

Notation of Section 5, as in the article:

* $\mathsf Z_n(t)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z}\mathfrak z_n(t,x)\delta_{y_n(t,x)}$ is the measure (3.5) (`tiltedMeasure`), with $\mathfrak z_n$ of (3.3) and $y_n(t,x)=n^{-1/2}(x-n^{3/4}t)$ of (3.4). For a function $\varphi$, $\mathsf Z_n(t;\varphi)=\sum_x\mathfrak z_n(t,x)\varphi(y_n(t,x))$ (`pairZ`), and $\mathsf W_n(t)=\mathsf Z_n(t;1)$. (In `docs/stage2-statements.md` this measure was written $\mathcal Z_n(t)$; here, as in the article, $\mathcal Z_n(t)$ is the measure (1.15) of Theorem 1.2, and $\mathcal Z_n(t)=\varrho_n(t)\mathsf Z_n(t)$ by (3.8).)
* $e_b(y)=e^{by}$ and $\cosh=\frac12(e_1+e_{-1})$.
* $\mathcal M_+=\mathcal M_+(\mathbb R)$ is the space of finite nonnegative measures on $\mathbb R$ with the topology of weak convergence (Mathlib's `FiniteMeasure ℝ`, as in the frozen statement of Theorem 1.2).
* $C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ is the space of continuous maps $[0,T]\to\mathcal M_+$ with the topology of uniform convergence (in Lean the compact-open topology, which is the same since $[0,T]$ is compact) and its **Borel** $\sigma$-algebra. This is the space, topology and $\sigma$-algebra of the frozen statement of Theorem 1.2. Mathlib's own $\sigma$-algebra on $\mathcal M_+$ (generated by the maps $\mu\mapsto\mu(A)$) is never used in the statements.
* A **version** of the process $t\mapsto\mathsf Z_n(t)$, $t\in[0,T]$, is a map $Z:\Omega\to C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ such that, almost surely, $Z(\omega)(t)=\mathsf Z_n(t)$ for all $t\in[0,T]$ simultaneously. Two versions are almost surely equal.
* $\lVert\varphi\rVert_{1,3}$, for a function $\varphi\in C^3_b(\mathbb R)$ independent of time, is the norm of Section 3.2: $\lVert\varphi\rVert_\infty+\lVert\varphi'\rVert_\infty+\lVert\varphi''\rVert_\infty+\lVert\varphi'''\rVert_\infty$. As in Stage 2, "$\lVert\varphi\rVert_{1,3}\le C$" is a hypothesis and the bound is stated with $C$.
* **Stopping times** in (5.2) are stopping times of $\mathcal F^n$ augmented by the null sets (decision D4 of Stage 2, already used in Proposition 4.3): slightly more stopping times than in the article, hence a slightly stronger statement.
* **Outer probability.** When an event is not claimed to be measurable, its probability is the outer probability. A bound on the outer probability is stronger than the same bound for a measurable event.

## Statements

### Notation $\mathsf Z_n(t;\varphi)=\int\varphi d\mathsf Z_n(t)$ (`Tightness/Integral.lean`)

* `integral_tiltedMeasure`, **notation of Section 5** ($\mu(\varphi)=\int\varphi d\mu$): for a realization $\omega$ and a time $t$ at which $\sum_x\mathfrak z_n(t,x)$ converges, and for every measurable function $\varphi$ with $\lvert\varphi\rvert\le c$: the measure $\mathsf Z_n(t)$ is finite, $\varphi$ is $\mathsf Z_n(t)$-integrable, and
$$\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi(y)\mathsf Z_n(t)(dy)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z}\mathfrak z_n(t,x)\varphi(y_n(t,x))=\mathsf Z_n(t;\varphi).$$
  This links the measure (3.5) to the series used in Sections 3 to 6; the article uses the two notations interchangeably.

### Lemma 5.2 (`Tightness/ExpMoments.lean`)

* `lemma_5_2`, **Lemma 5.2, with (5.1)**: for $n\ge1$,
  * almost surely, for all $t\ge0$ and $b\in[-1,1]$, the series $\mathsf Z_n(t;e_b)=\sum_x\mathfrak z_n(t,x)e^{by_n(t,x)}$ converges (hence also $\mathsf Z_n(t;\cosh)$);
  * for $b\in[-1,1]$ and $t\ge0$, $\mathsf Z_n(t;e_b)$ is integrable and $\mathbb E[\mathsf Z_n(t;e_b)]\le e^{2t}$;
  * (5.1): for $T>0$ and $K>0$,
$$\mathbb P\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathsf Z_n(t;\cosh)\ge K\Big)\le\frac{2e^{2T}}K,$$
  the supremum being taken in $[0,\infty]$ and the probability being the outer probability.

### Equation (5.2) (`Tightness/AldousCondition.lean`)

* `aldous_condition`, **equation (5.2)** (Aldous' condition), in the quantitative form given at the end of the proof of Proposition 5.1: for every $T$ there is a constant $c$ (depending only on $T$) such that, for every probability space, Brownian family and wave function, every $n\ge1$, every $\varphi\in C^3_b(\mathbb R)$ with $\lVert\varphi\rVert_{1,3}\le C$, every stopping time $\tau\le T$ of the augmented filtration $\mathcal F^n$, every $s\in[0,\frac1{16}]$ and every $\eta>0$:
  * almost surely, $\sum_x\mathfrak z_n(t,x)$ converges for all $t\ge0$;
  * the bound
$$\mathbb P\Big(\big\lvert\mathsf Z_n(\tau+s;\varphi)-\mathsf Z_n(\tau;\varphi)\big\rvert>\eta\Big)\le c\Big(\frac{Cs}\eta+\frac{C^2\sqrt s}{\eta^2}\Big)\quad\text{(outer probability)}.$$

  Taking the supremum over $n$, $\tau$ and $s\le\delta$ and letting $\delta\to0$ gives (5.2) as printed. The article's proof gives this bound with $2C\delta/\eta+4C\sqrt\delta/\eta^2$, its constant $C$ containing $\lVert\varphi\rVert_{1,3}$ and $\lVert\varphi\rVert_{1,3}^2$ and the bounds (4.8), (4.9) on $[0,T+1]$.

### Proposition 5.1 (`Tightness/Prop51.lean`)

* `prop_5_1_continuous`, **Proposition 5.1, continuity of the trajectories**: for $n\ge1$, almost surely there is a continuous map $\gamma:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal M_+$ with $\gamma(t)=\mathsf Z_n(t)$ for every $t\ge0$.

* `prop_5_1_version`, **Proposition 5.1, the process as a random variable in $C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$**: for $n\ge1$ and every $T$, there is a version $Z:\Omega\to C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ of $t\mapsto\mathsf Z_n(t)$ which is measurable for the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of the topology of uniform convergence.

* `prop_5_1`, **Proposition 5.1, tightness**: for $T>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there is a compact set $\mathcal K\subset C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ such that, for every probability space, Brownian family and wave function, every $n\ge1$ and every version $Z$ of $t\mapsto\mathsf Z_n(t)$ on $[0,T]$,
$$\mathbb P(Z\notin\mathcal K)\le\varepsilon$$
  (outer probability if $Z$ is not measurable).

* `prop_5_1_isTight`, **Proposition 5.1, tightness of the laws**: let $T>0$ and, for each $k\in\mathbb N$, a number $n_k\ge1$, a probability space with a Brownian family and a wave function, and an almost everywhere measurable version $Z_k$ of $t\mapsto\mathsf Z_{n_k}(t)$ on $[0,T]$. Then the family of the laws of the $Z_k$ on $C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ is tight, in the sense of Mathlib (`IsTightMeasureSet`): for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a compact $\mathcal K$ with $\mathbb P(Z_k\notin\mathcal K)\le\varepsilon$ for all $k$. The measurability hypothesis is what makes the laws genuine (for a map that is not almost everywhere measurable, Lean's image measure is an arbitrary Dirac mass, not the law).

## Deviations from the article

Decisions of `docs/stage3-design.md` (Section 5), all applied as recommended there. The author has not yet ruled on them; if one changes, the statements will be revised.

1. **D1'. No Skorokhod space.** The article applies Jakubowski's criterion in $D([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ and then notes that the paths are continuous. Mathlib has no Skorokhod space; the Lean proof will use a criterion stated directly in $C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ (compact containment, plus tightness of the real processes $\mathsf Z_n(\cdot;f_j)$ for a countable family $f_j$). This changes the proof, not the statements: `prop_5_1` and `prop_5_1_isTight` are tightness in $C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$, the conclusion of the article. *Recommended because* it is the route of `PLAN.md` and avoids building the Skorokhod space.
2. **D2'. Countable family of test functions** ($f_0=1$ and $x\mapsto\beta(mx-k)$, $m\ge1$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, for a smooth bump $\beta$ whose integer translates sum to $1$), instead of all $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$. Only the proof is affected; no statement mentions the family. *Recommended because* a countable family makes the union bound over test functions possible and gives the metrizability and separability of $\mathcal M_+$, which Mathlib does not have.
3. **D3'. What "tight" means.** The probability spaces vary with the realization of the model, so the family of laws is not a family of measures on one space indexed by $n$ alone. The statement is therefore the uniform one (`prop_5_1`: one compact set for all realizations, all $n\ge1$, all versions), plus, for a sequence of realizations as in Theorem 1.2, Mathlib's `IsTightMeasureSet` of the laws (`prop_5_1_isTight`). *Recommended because* the first is the strongest natural form, and the second is the literal "the family of laws is tight", the form used by Prokhorov's theorem in Section 7.
4. **D4'. (5.2) in quantitative form**, with $\varphi\in C^3_b(\mathbb R)$, $\lVert\varphi\rVert_{1,3}\le C$ (the article takes $\varphi\in C_c^\infty$; its proof only uses $\lVert\varphi\rVert_{1,3}$), stopping times of the augmented filtration, and a constant $c$ depending only on $T$, stated as "there is $c$" (an explicit value such as $2^{20+16T}$ would also be possible). *Recommended because* the tightness proof needs the bound in quantitative form (two time scales), and the test functions of D2' are in $C^3_b$, not in $C_c^\infty$ for $f_0=1$.
5. **D5'. Lemma 5.2**: the supremum in (5.1) is taken in $[0,\infty]$ and the probability is the outer one; the almost sure convergence of $\sum_x\mathfrak z_n(t,x)e^{by_n(t,x)}$ and the integrability of $\mathsf Z_n(t;e_b)$ are asserted. *Recommended because* $e_b$ and $\cosh$ are unbounded, so without the convergence conjunct the statement could hold because a divergent series is $0$ in Lean.
6. **D6'. Continuity and versions.** "Continuous trajectories in $\mathcal M_+$" is rendered as: almost surely, there is a continuous $\gamma:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal M_+$ equal to $\mathsf Z_n(t)$ for every $t$ (`prop_5_1_continuous`); and a measurable version with values in $C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ exists (`prop_5_1_version`), measurable for the Borel $\sigma$-algebra, as in the frozen statement. *Recommended because* the frozen Theorem 1.2 is stated with such versions, and the measurability of the version is not automatic (it is not stated in the article).
7. **D7'. Scope: $\mathsf Z_n$ only**, as in the article. No tightness statement is made for the measures $\mathcal Z_n=\varrho_n\mathsf Z_n$ of Theorem 1.2; the passage from $\mathsf Z_n$ to $\mathcal Z_n$ belongs to Stages 4 and 5 (last line of Section 7). *Recommended because* it follows the article.
8. **D8'. Polish space.** The article says that $\mathcal M_+$ is Polish. Stage 3 will prove that it is metrizable and separable (needed for the measurability of versions and for Section 7), but not that it is complete; no statement of this list asserts completeness. *Recommended because* Skorokhod's representation theorem, where the article uses the Polish property, holds in separable metric spaces. *But note* (independent audit): the route of `PLAN.md` for the end of Section 7 also uses the Lusin–Souslin theorem on $C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$, which needs a Polish space; so completeness of $\mathcal M_+$ (or another argument) will probably be needed in Stage 4, at an estimated cost of a few hundred lines. The decision concerns only the order of the work: no statement of Stage 3 depends on it.

Other deviations:

9. **Notation lemma.** `integral_tiltedMeasure` (the identity $\int\varphi d\mathsf Z_n(t)=\mathsf Z_n(t;\varphi)$) is stated explicitly; the article uses it silently.
10. **Safeguard conjuncts.** `aldous_condition` asserts the almost sure convergence of $\sum_x\mathfrak z_n(t,x)$ for all $t\ge0$ (as in all Stage 2 statements), and `prop_5_1_isTight` requires the versions to be almost everywhere measurable (otherwise their laws would be $0$ in Lean).
11. **Versions.** `prop_5_1` is stated for every version, measurable or not (outer probability), so that later stages can use any version; since two versions are almost surely equal, this is not stronger than the statement for one version.
12. **Universe.** In `prop_5_1`, the probability spaces range over a fixed (arbitrary) universe of types; this is a technicality of Lean without mathematical content.

## Remarks on the article

No error was found. Minor points, none of which changes a statement:

* Lemma 5.2 uses $\mathsf Z_n(t;e_b)$ and $\mathsf Z_n(t;\cosh)$ for unbounded functions; the convergence of these series follows from (2.2) (with exponent $a=2$) and is not stated in the article.
