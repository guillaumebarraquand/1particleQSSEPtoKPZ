## How to read the statements

The conventions of Stages 2 and 3 apply (`docs/stage2-statements.md`, `docs/stage3-statements.md`): arbitrary probability spaces, filtrations without the usual conditions, "martingale" and "bracket" in the sense of `IsCL2Mart` and `HasBracket` (continuous square-integrable martingale on $[0,T]$, up to null sets; the bracket is integrable, continuous, nondecreasing, $0$ at $0$, and $M^2-A$ is a martingale); every series, integral and law that appears is asserted to be genuine; probabilities of events that are not claimed measurable are outer probabilities. The universes of the probability spaces are arbitrary.

* $\mathcal M_+=\mathcal M_+(\mathbb R)$ is the space of finite nonnegative measures with the weak topology, and $C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ has the topology of uniform convergence and its **Borel** $\sigma$-algebra (as in the frozen Theorem 1.2). For a path $\gamma$ and a real $t$, $\gamma(t)$ means $\gamma$ at the projection of $t$ on $[0,T]$; $\mu(f)=\int f\,d\mu$.
* **White noise and stochastic integral**: the frozen definitions of Stage 0. `IsWhiteNoise` describes $\xi$ through its distribution function $S$ on all of $\mathbb R_+\times\mathbb R$ (rectangle masses $\xi((s,t]\times(a,b])$ centred Gaussian with variance $(t-s)(b-a)$, independent for disjoint space intervals and independent of $\mathcal G_s$). `IsWalshIntegral` "$X=\int h\,d\xi$" is Walsh's integral (smallest relation containing the elementary predictable fields, stable under sums and $L^2$ limits); writing it in a conclusion asserts that the integral exists.
* **Natural filtration** of a random path $\mathcal Z$: $\mathcal G^0_t=\sigma(\mathcal Z(s):s\le t)$ (`pathFiltration`); "$\mathcal G^0$ with the null sets" is $\mathcal G^0_t\vee\sigma(\text{null sets})$. The article uses the right-continuous completion $\mathcal G_t$; for continuous martingales the two give the same notion (a continuous martingale for $\mathcal G^0$ with the null sets is one for $\mathcal G$, and conversely if adapted to $\mathcal G^0$).
* **Predictable**: measurable for the predictable $\sigma$-algebra on $\mathbb R_+\times\Omega$ (Mathlib's, generated by $(s,\infty)\times A$, $A\in\mathcal G_s$, and $\lbrace0\rbrace\times A$, $A\in\mathcal G_0$) times the Borel $\sigma$-algebra in $x$.
* $C^{1,3}_b([0,T]\times\mathbb R)$ is `IsC13` of Stage 2 (time derivative given explicitly, one-sided at $0$ and $T$); $C^\infty_c(\mathbb R)$ is "smooth with compact support".
* **Mollifier** $\rho$: smooth, even, nonnegative, supported in $[-1,1]$, integral $1$ (Section 6); $\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\rho(x/\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$; $\lVert(\varphi\mu)\ast\rho_\varepsilon\rVert_2^2$ is `smoothedNormSq` of Stage 2.
* **Versions.** As in Stage 3: $\mathsf Z_n(t)$ is the measure (3.5); a version of $(\mathsf Z_n(t))_{t\in[0,T]}$ is a map $\Omega\to C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ equal almost surely to $\mathsf Z_n(t)$ for all $t\in[0,T]$ at once.

## Definitions (`LimitPoints/Defs.lean`, `Uniqueness/Defs.lean`)

* `limMart`: for a random path $\mathcal Z$ and $\phi\in C^{1,3}_b$ with time derivative $\partial_s\phi$, the process (7.2)
$$M^\phi(t)=\mathcal Z(t;\phi_t)-\phi_0(0)-\int_0^t\mathcal Z\Big(s;\partial_s\phi_s+\tfrac12\partial_x^2\phi_s\Big)ds.$$
* `limBracket`: $\int_0^t\int_{\mathbb R}Z(s,x)^2\phi_s(x)^2\,dx\,ds$, the right side of (7.3).
* `limDensity`, `limField`: $Z(t,x)=\liminf_{k\to\infty}(\mathcal Z(t)\ast\rho_{1/k})(x)$ if finite, $0$ otherwise, as in the proof of Proposition 7.1 (2); $(\mu\ast\rho_\varepsilon)(x)=\int\rho_\varepsilon(x-y)\mu(dy)$. (In Lean the sequence is indexed by $k+1$, $k\ge0$.)
* `heatConv`: $(g_r\ast f)(y)=\int g_r(y-x)f(x)\,dx$, with $g$ the heat kernel of (1.13).
* `IsWeakMild` ("$Z\in\mathcal H_T$ satisfies (7.13)"): $(\omega,t,x)\mapsto Z(t,x)$ is jointly measurable; $\mathbb E\int_0^T\int Z(t,x)^2dx\,dt<\infty$; and for almost every $t\in(0,T]$ (one negligible set for all $f$), for every $f\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb R)$, almost surely
$$\int Z(t,x)f(x)\,dx=\int g_t(x)f(x)\,dx+\int_0^t\int_{\mathbb R}(g_{t-s}\ast f)(y)\,Z(s,y)\,\xi(ds\,dy).\qquad(7.13)$$
  Predictability is not required (decision D8″).

## Statements

### Proposition 7.1 (`LimitPoints/Prop71.lean`)

* `prop_7_1`, **Proposition 7.1 with (7.1), (7.2), (7.3)**. Let $\rho$ be a mollifier, $T>0$, $n_k\ge1$ with $n_k\to\infty$; for each $k$, a probability space with a Brownian family, a wave function on $\mathbb Z$, and a version $\mathsf Z_{n_k}$ of $(\mathsf Z_{n_k}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}$. Let $\mathcal Z:\Omega\to C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ be measurable (on any probability space) with $\mathsf Z_{n_k}\to\mathcal Z$ in distribution. Then:
  1. almost surely $\mathcal Z(0)=\delta_0$;
  2. the field $Z$ (`limField`) is predictable for $\mathcal G^0$ (without null sets), nonnegative, satisfies (7.1) $\mathbb E\int_0^T\int_{\mathbb R}Z(t,x)^2dx\,dt<\infty$, and almost surely $\mathcal Z(t)(dx)=Z(t,x)\,dx$ for almost every $t\in[0,T]$;
  3. for every $\phi\in C^{1,3}_b([0,T]\times\mathbb R)$, $M^\phi$ (7.2) is a continuous square-integrable martingale for $\mathcal G^0$ with the null sets, with $\langle M^\phi\rangle_t=\int_0^t\int Z(s,x)^2\phi_s(x)^2dx\,ds$ (7.3).

  "Limit point" is rendered (decision D6″) by: any random path whose law is the limit in distribution of the laws of versions of $\mathsf Z_{n_k}$, for arbitrary realizations, one for each $k$. The statement concerns the law of $\mathcal Z$ only; the Skorokhod representation is inside the proof.

* `eq_7_4`, **equation (7.4)**: there is a constant $C$, depending only on $\rho$, such that for every limit point $\mathcal Z$ as above (any $T>0$, any $n_k$, realizations, versions, and any probability space carrying $\mathcal Z$: in Lean the space is quantified after $C$; only its universe level is fixed beforehand, which is how Lean quantifies over all types) and every $\varepsilon>0$,
$$\mathbb E\int_0^T\lVert\mathcal Z(t)\ast\rho_\varepsilon\rVert_2^2\,dt\le C\,2^{16T},$$
  computed in $[0,\infty]$, the integrand being asserted jointly measurable in $(\omega,t)$.

### Proposition 7.2 (`WhiteNoise/Prop72.lean`)

* `prop_7_2`, **Proposition 7.2 with (7.8)**. Let $\mathcal Z:\Omega\to C([0,T],\mathcal M_+)$ be measurable with $\mathcal Z(0)=\delta_0$ almost surely, $T>0$, $\mathcal G$ a filtration, and $Z\ge0$ a $\mathcal G$-predictable field with (7.1), such that almost surely $\mathcal Z(t)(dx)=Z(t,x)dx$ for almost every $t\in[0,T]$, and such that for every $\phi\in C^{1,3}_b$, $M^\phi$ is a continuous square-integrable $\mathcal G$-martingale with bracket (7.3). Let $\xi''$ be a white noise (for some filtration) on another probability space $\Omega''$. Then on $\Omega\times\Omega''$ with the product measure there are a filtration $\bar{\mathcal G}\supseteq\mathcal G$ and a white noise $\xi$ for $\bar{\mathcal G}$ such that for all $f\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb R)$ and $t\in(0,T]$, almost surely,
$$\mathcal Z(t;f)=\int g_t(x)f(x)\,dx+\int_0^t\int_{\mathbb R}(g_{t-s}\ast f)(y)\,Z(s,y)\,\xi(ds\,dy).\qquad(7.8)$$

  "Let $\mathcal Z$ and $Z$ be as in Proposition 7.1" is rendered by the conclusions (1)–(3) of Proposition 7.1 as hypotheses (the condition $\mathcal Z(0)=\delta_0$ of (1) is used in Step 3 of the article's proof, where it gives $M^\phi(0)=0$; without it the statement is false: $\mathcal Z\equiv0$ and $Z\equiv0$ satisfy the other hypotheses, $M^\phi\equiv-\phi_0(0)$ being a martingale with bracket $0$, but (7.8) would give $\int g_tf=0$), for an arbitrary filtration (so the statement does not mention the QSSEP); "possibly after enlarging the probability space" by the product with an arbitrary space carrying a white noise (decision D7″). The white noise is defined on all of $\mathbb R_+\times\mathbb R$, as the frozen definition requires (the article: on $[0,T]\times\mathbb R$).

### Proposition 7.3 (`Uniqueness/Prop73.lean`)

* `prop_7_3`, **Proposition 7.3 with (7.13)**. Let $\xi$ be a white noise for a filtration $\bar{\mathcal G}$, $T>0$, and $Z^1,Z^2$ two fields satisfying `IsWeakMild` (jointly measurable, (7.1), (7.13) for almost every $t$ and all $f\in C^\infty_c$). ### The solution of the stochastic heat equation (`Uniqueness/MildSolution.lean`)

These concern the field $Z$ **given** in the statement of Theorem 1.2: a solution of (1.13) (the frozen `IsMildSolution`, continuous on $(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R$) driven by a white noise. The existence theorem of Chen–Dalang, used in the article for $Z^\star$ of (7.14), is not used (decision D4″).

* `IsMildSolution.integral_sq`, **equation (7.15), in series form** (decision D9″): for $t>0$ and $x\in\mathbb R$, $Z(t,x)\in L^2$ and
$$\mathbb E[Z(t,x)^2]=\sum_{k\ge1}K^{\ast k}(t,x),\qquad K^{\ast k}(t,x)=\frac{2^{-k}t^{k/2-1}}{\Gamma(k/2)}\,g_{t/2}(x),$$
  the series being asserted to converge. Here $K(t,x)=g_t(x)^2$ and $K^{\ast k}$ is its $k$-th space-time convolution power; the sum of the series is the right side of (7.15) ($\sum_{k\ge1}K^{\ast k}=g_t(x)^2\big(1+\sqrt{\pi t}\,e^{t/4}\Phi(\sqrt{t/2})\big)$, $\Phi$ the standard Gaussian distribution function, a Mittag-Leffler identity: checked by hand by the design agent, and numerically here for $t=0.1,1,3$ to 15 digits; the closed form is not stated in Lean).
* `IsMildSolution.isWeakMild`, **after (7.15)** ("$Z^\star\in\mathcal H_T$" and "$Z^\star$ satisfies (7.13) for all $t\in(0,T]$", stochastic Fubini): for $T>0$ there is a jointly measurable field $Z'$, almost surely equal to $Z$ on $(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R$, which satisfies `IsWeakMild` and (7.13) for **every** $t\in(0,T]$ and every $f\in C^\infty_c$.

## Deviations from the article

1. **Limit points** (D6″): stated for any random path whose law is a limit in distribution of laws of versions of $\mathsf Z_{n_k}$, with arbitrary realizations (one per $k$), not "along a subsequence of the family $(\mathsf Z_n)$".
2. **Filtrations**: $\mathcal G^0$ with the null sets instead of the right-continuous completion $\mathcal G$ (equivalent for continuous martingales); in Proposition 7.2 the filtration $\bar{\mathcal G}$ need not satisfy the usual conditions.
3. **Predictability in Proposition 7.1 (2)** is asserted for $\mathcal G^0$ itself (stronger than for $\mathcal G$).
4. **$\mathcal H_T$** in Proposition 7.3 does not require predictability (D8″): the hypotheses are weaker, the statement stronger.
5. **Proposition 7.2** in the form D7″ (hypotheses = conclusions (1)–(3) of 7.1 for any filtration; enlargement = product with a space carrying a white noise; the noise on all of $\mathbb R_+\times\mathbb R$).
6. **(7.15)** in series form (D9″); **(7.14)** is not stated: the field of Theorem 1.2 is used instead of $Z^\star$ (D4″).
7. **(7.4)** also asserts the joint measurability of the integrand (so that the expectation is a genuine integral).
8. **$\liminf_k(\mathcal Z(t)\ast\rho_{1/k})$** is indexed by $1/(k+1)$, $k\ge0$; immaterial.
9. **Theorem 1.2 for $L_n=\infty$** is `main_theorem` with $L_k=\infty$ (D11″), measures $\mathcal Z_n$ of (1.15) (not $\mathsf Z_n$), $\mathcal Z(t)=\max(Z,0)\,dx$, on the given space carrying the given $Z$.

## Decisions D1″–D11″ (from `docs/stage4-design.md`, Section 5; recommendation in bold)

1. **D1″. Skorokhod's representation along a subsequence**, proved by explicit couplings on a separable metrizable space, instead of Billingsley's Theorem 6.7 on a Polish space (the article only uses subsequences). **Recommended.**
2. **D2″. No Polishness of $\mathcal M_+$**: the Lusin–Souslin step is replaced by the $\sigma$-compactness of a tight law (the map $\gamma\mapsto(\gamma(t)(f_j))_{t,j}$ is a homeomorphism on each compact set). Alternative: prove completeness of $\mathcal M_+$ (500–800 lines). **Recommended: no Polishness.**
3. **D3″. BDG replaced by a localization** at the first time $\max(\langle M_n^\phi\rangle,(M_n^\phi)^2)\ge L$; the passage from local to true martingale is written out. **Recommended.**
4. **D4″. Identification without existence**: uniqueness in law for (7.13) by conditional expectation onto the noise, Proposition 7.3 and transfer between probability spaces; the given $Z$ plays the role of $Z^\star$; existence, continuity and positivity of $Z^\star$ are not used. **Recommended.**
5. **D5″. Proof of Proposition 7.3** with the test functions $\rho_\delta(z-\cdot)$ and the inequality $F\le K\ast F$ instead of an orthonormal basis and Parseval (statement unchanged). **Recommended.**
6. **D6″. Form of Proposition 7.1**: any $Y$ whose law is the limit; natural filtration with null sets; martingale problem as `HasBracket`. **Recommended.**
7. **D7″. Form of Proposition 7.2**: hypotheses = conclusions of 7.1 for an arbitrary filtration; enlargement = product with a given white noise (in the proof of Theorem 1.2: the white noise of the statement). Alternative: prove E2 first and state "there is an enlargement". **Recommended.**
8. **D8″. $\mathcal H_T$** in Proposition 7.3 without predictability. **Recommended.**
9. **D9″. (7.15)** as the series $\sum_kK^{\ast k}$, not the closed form (+200–400 lines). **Recommended.**
10. **D10″. Companions in Stage 4**: W and U3 yes; E2 optional; P in Stage 5 (needs E1), Stage 4 giving the conditional form. **Recommended: W, U3 in Stage 4; E2 if usage allows; P in Stage 5.**

## Remarks on the article

No error was found. Points where the Lean statements make something explicit:

* Proposition 7.1 (3) ends with "$(M^\phi)^2-Q$ is a local martingale ... this proves (7.3)"; `HasBracket` requires a true martingale, which follows from $\mathbb E[Q(T)]<\infty$ by (7.1) and Doob's inequality.
* In the proof of Theorem 1.2, "the law of $\mathcal Z$ is the law of the process described in the statement" uses that $Z^\star$ is a function of the noise, so that its law does not depend on the probability space; in Lean this is a separate step (uniqueness in law for (7.13)).
* The estimates used in Section 7.1 are applied to realizations that change with $n$; the constants of Sections 3–6 do not depend on the realization (Proposition 6.2 is stated in Stage 2 for a fixed realization, and its uniform version will be derived from its proof).
* After (7.15) the article says that $Z^\star$ satisfies (7.13) for all $t\in(0,T]$; `IsMildSolution.isWeakMild` asserts this for the given $Z$.

## Corrections of 13 November 2026

An independent audit (`docs/stage4-audit.md`) and an external review found the following; all are corrected in the Lean files.

1. **`prop_7_2` was false**: it did not assume $\mathcal Z(0)=\delta_0$ (counterexample $\mathcal Z\equiv0$, $Z\equiv0$ above; checked by the Lean kernel in the audit). It now assumes it (hypothesis `hY0`), as the article does ("$\mathcal Z$ and $Z$ as in Proposition 7.1"). `prop_7_1` gives it, so the route is unchanged.
2. **`eq_7_4`**: the Lean statement let $C$ depend on the probability space of $\mathcal Z$ (bound before `∃ C`). Now the space is bound after `∃ C`: $C$ depends only on $\rho$, as the article and this sheet say.
3. Not statements of the article (no approval needed; listed in `docs/stage4-interfaces.md`, departures 19–22): the projection onto the noise used in the identification of the limit was defined with a conditional expectation for $\nu\otimes\mathrm{Leb}^2$, which Lean sets to $0$ for square-integrable but non-integrable fields; it is now a conditional expectation for the probability measure $\nu\otimes\gamma\otimes\gamma$ ($\gamma$ the standard Gaussian), which equals the orthogonal projection in $L^2(\nu\otimes\mathrm{Leb}^2)$. The closure rule of the integral against the martingale measure ($h\cdot M$, Step 1 of Proposition 7.2) now requires the limit process to be measurable (up to null sets) at each time. A lemma passing from (7.8) to (7.13) on the enlarged space was added (`isWeakMild_prod_of_eq_7_8`). The uniform form of Proposition 6.2 was checked against the Stage 2 proof (constant of Lemma 6.4, depending only on $\rho$).
4. **Parenthesization of (7.13)** (found by task K1 and confirmed by a parse audit of all 161 approved declarations of Stages 0–4, `docs/parse-audit.md`, 13 November 2026): in `IsWeakMild` and `IsMildSolution.isWeakMild`, the value of the stochastic integral was written `∫ x, Z t x ω * f x - ∫ x, heatKernel t x * f x`, which Lean reads as $\int\big(Z(t,x)f(x)-\int g_tf\big)dx$ (the binder `∫ x,` extends to the end of the expression), not as $\int Z(t,x)f(x)dx-\int g_t(x)f(x)dx$. With that reading `IsWeakMild` would hold only for fields that vanish almost everywhere and `IsMildSolution.isWeakMild` would be false. The parentheses were added (`(∫ x, Z t x ω * f x) - ∫ x, heatKernel t x * f x`), with the author's agreement (13 November 2026): the Lean now says (7.13) as written on this sheet. No other approved declaration has an error of this kind.
